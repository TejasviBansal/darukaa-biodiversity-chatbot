SOURCE: FAO Service Functions of Agroforestry Systems

---
Agroforestry systems can support water regulation by increasing infiltration,
reducing surface runoff, improving soil cover, and moderating microclimate.
FAO reviews agroforestry service functions including soil fertility maintenance,
erosion reduction, water conservation, carbon capture, and biodiversity
conservation in fragmented landscapes. Trees and shrubs contribute to these
functions through roots, litter, canopy interception, and long-term biomass.

The hydrological outcome depends on context. In humid or erosion-prone sites,
tree cover can reduce sediment movement and help rainfall enter the soil. In
dryland systems, trees may compete with crops for water if spacing and species
selection are poorly matched to rainfall. Well-designed agroforestry therefore
uses local knowledge of soil depth, rainfall timing, rooting patterns, and crop
shade tolerance. Pruning, contour layout, and mulched tree rows can reduce
competition and improve complementarity. At landscape scale, agroforestry can
also improve ecological connectivity, especially where tree lines, hedgerows,
or scattered shade trees link remnant habitats. These benefits build slowly as
woody vegetation matures, so early monitoring should track survival, ground
cover, crop performance, and soil moisture rather than expecting immediate full
system recovery.
