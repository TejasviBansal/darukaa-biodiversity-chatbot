INTERVENTION: intercropping
METRIC: pest_pressure
SOURCE: FAO Conservation Agriculture Species Diversification

REGION: general
---
Intercropping increases plant diversity within the same field during the same
season. FAO conservation agriculture guidance emphasizes diversification of crop
species grown in sequences or associations because different plant species feed
different soil organisms, use resources in different ways, and can interrupt
crop-specific pest and disease cycles. In practice, the pest benefit of
intercropping depends on crop choice, planting geometry, and whether the
secondary crop provides habitat for beneficial insects or simply competes with
the main crop.

Well-planned intercrops can reduce pest pressure by making host plants less
continuous, adding floral resources, and supporting predators or parasitoids.
Legume-cereal systems may also improve nitrogen cycling, while deep- and
shallow-rooted combinations can explore different soil layers. These benefits
are not guaranteed. Poorly matched mixtures can increase humidity, complicate
weed control, or make harvest difficult. Intercropping works best when each crop
has a clear role: soil cover, nitrogen contribution, market product, fodder,
pollinator resource, or erosion protection. Local field trials are important
because varieties, rainfall, and labor constraints strongly affect whether the
association improves resilience.
