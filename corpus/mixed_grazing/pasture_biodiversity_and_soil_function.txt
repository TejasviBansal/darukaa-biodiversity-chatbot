Mixed grazing and diverse pasture management can support biodiversity when they
increase plant functional diversity and avoid chronic overgrazing. USDA NRCS
pastureland soil health guidance emphasizes optimizing disturbance, soil cover,
biodiversity, and continuous living roots. Diverse forage mixtures can provide
different rooting depths, seasonal growth patterns, and habitat resources for
soil organisms, insects, and wildlife.

The ecological benefit depends more on grazing management than on the mere
presence of multiple livestock species. Grazing intensity, timing, recovery
periods, water placement, and shade access determine whether vegetation
diversity increases or declines. Well-managed grazing can stimulate regrowth,
cycle nutrients through manure, and maintain cover. Overgrazing does the
opposite: it reduces infiltration, exposes soil, simplifies vegetation, and can
increase runoff. Mixed grazing may help use forage more evenly because different
animals prefer different plants, but stocking rate must remain matched to
forage supply. Monitoring should track ground cover, plant species balance,
bare soil, manure distribution, and recovery after grazing. The goal is a
pasture system with living roots, protective cover, and resilient forage
communities.
