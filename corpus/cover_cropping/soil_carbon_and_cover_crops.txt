METRIC: soil_organic_carbon
SOURCE: FAO Soil Organic Cover in Conservation Agriculture
SOURCE_URL: https://www.fao.org/conservation-agriculture/in-practice/soil-organic-cover/en/
REGION: general
---
Cover crops are grown during periods when commercial crops are absent, and their
main biodiversity value begins with keeping living roots and plant residues in
the system for more of the year. FAO describes soil organic cover as a core part
of conservation agriculture because residues and cover crops protect soil,
recycle nutrients, improve structure, and add organic matter. The soil organic
carbon response depends strongly on climate, biomass production, residue
management, grazing pressure, and whether tillage is reduced at the same time.
For that reason, a single universal percentage gain should not be assumed.

In degraded cropland, cover crops can gradually improve carbon inputs through
roots, shoots, and microbial activity. Legume cover crops add biologically fixed
nitrogen, while grasses often produce persistent residues that protect the soil
surface. Mixed cover crops can combine these functions. The practice is most
reliable when cover crops are planned as part of a rotation rather than as a
standalone add-on. In dryland systems, water trade-offs must be managed carefully
so that cover crops build soil function without depleting moisture needed by the
next cash crop. FAO conservation agriculture examples report an average carbon
accumulation of about 0.5 tonne per hectare per year in southern Brazil, while
also stressing that zero tillage alone is not enough without large biomass
inputs from residues, rotations, and cover crops.
