Cover crops reduce erosion risk by shielding bare soil from raindrop impact,
slowing surface runoff, and holding soil in place with roots. USDA NRCS describes
cover crops as non-cash crops grown during otherwise bare periods to protect,
stabilize, and enrich the soil. This protection is especially important after
harvest, during fallow periods, and in fields where low residue cover leaves the
surface exposed to wind or water erosion.

The water benefits of cover crops are closely tied to soil structure. As roots
grow and residues decompose, soil aggregation can improve, which allows more
rainfall to enter the profile instead of running off the surface. Better
infiltration can reduce sediment movement and help crops withstand short dry
spells. These outcomes vary by species, planting window, biomass level, soil
texture, and termination timing. A dense cover crop may provide strong erosion
control, but in water-limited regions it should be matched to seasonal rainfall
and the water needs of the following crop. The strongest benefits generally come
when cover crops are paired with residue retention, reduced disturbance, and
diverse rotations. SARE summarizes field evidence showing non-legume cover crops
reduced soil loss by about 31-100 percent compared with no cover crop, while
legume cover crops reduced soil loss by about 38-69 percent.
